---
title: Depreciation
---
Depreciation is the accounting recognition that most physical assets lose value over time due to wear, tear, and obsolescence.

This is where insurance meets the cold, hard reality of accounting. Your laptop isn't worth what you paid for it two years ago. Your roof has been slowly baking in the sun. Depreciation is the number that quantifies that loss in value. And it's crucially important for insurance because of the difference between [[Actual Cash Value]] and [[Replacement Cost]]. ACV is "what you could have sold the thing for right before it was destroyed," which is Replacement Cost minus Depreciation. It's why people are often shocked by a claims payout—they think of the value of a new one, but The Pool is only promising to indemnify you for the value of the old one you lost. Unless you bought the more expensive replacement cost coverage, which is a different bet entirely.
