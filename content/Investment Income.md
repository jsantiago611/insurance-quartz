---
title: Investment Income
---
Investment Income is the money an insurer makes by investing the [[Float]]—the pile of premium dollars it's holding but hasn't yet paid out in claims.

And this is, like, the secret sauce of the business. You collect premiums today. You pay claims over the next few years. In the meantime, you have this giant pool of money just sitting there. The [[Float]]. So you invest it in bonds, maybe some stocks. The income from those investments is critical. For some insurers, especially in [[Life-Health Insurance]] where the float is long-term, the underwriting profit can be minimal or even a loss, and the real profit is the investment income. It's the business of getting paid to hold other people's money. It's a great business, if you can get it.
