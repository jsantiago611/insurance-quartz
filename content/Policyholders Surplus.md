---
title: Policyholders Surplus
---
Policyholders Surplus is the difference between an insurance company's [[Assets]] and its [[Liabilities]]. It's the net worth of [[The Pool]].

This is the cushion. This is the safety margin. The liabilities are all the future claims the company has promised to pay. The assets are what it has to pay them with. The surplus is what's left over. It's the capital that guarantees the company can pay all its claims even if losses are worse than expected. Regulators monitor this number obsessively because it's the key to [[Solvency]]. If the surplus gets too low, The Pool is in danger of running dry.
