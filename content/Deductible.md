---
title: Deductible
---
A Deductible is the part of a loss you agree to pay for yourself before [[The Pool]] starts paying. It's the "first dollars" of any claim.

And this is great, right? I mean, you're buying insurance to be made whole, to be [[Indemnify|indemnified]], so why would you want to pay for any of the loss yourself? Isn't a policy with a $0 deductible... strictly better?

Ehhhh, look. The simple, obvious-in-retrospect reason is that if you had a $0 deductible, you would file a claim for everything. The tiny little scratch on your car door from a shopping cart. The lost phone charger. The Pool would be drowning in a million tiny, administratively expensive claims—the cost of sending a [[Claims Representative]] out to look at that scratch might be more than the repair itself. The [[Expense Ratio]] would explode. So the deductible is there to stop you from bothering The Pool with small stuff. It's a friction mechanism.

But there's a deeper, more philosophical reason, which is [[Moral Hazard]]. If you know you are going to eat the first $500 or $1,000 of any loss, you have some skin in the game. You have an incentive to, you know, not park in the sketchy lot. To lock your doors. To be careful. The deductible aligns your interests with The Pool's interests. The Pool doesn't want to pay claims, and with a deductible, you don't really want to have a claim either, at least not a small one. It makes you a partner in risk management, sort of.

Still? It works. It's a brilliantly simple tool that makes the entire system more efficient by making it a little bit annoying to use.
