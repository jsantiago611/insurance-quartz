---
title: Balance Sheet
---

A [[Balance Sheet]] is a snapshot of a company's financial position at a point in time. For an insurer, it's a picture of [[The Pool]] (and how much trouble the insurance company is in).

It's based on the equation Assets = Liabilities + Policyholders' Surplus. The [[Assets]] are what they own, the [[Liabilities]] are mostly their best guess at future claims ([[Loss Reserves]]), and the [[Policyholders Surplus]] is what's left over—the cushion that proves they can probably pay you if your house burns down.

If we follow our pool analogy... On one side, you have [[Assets]]—all the stuff the insurer owns (bonds, stocks, cash). This is the water in The Pool. On the other side, you have [[Liabilities]]—mostly the reserves set aside to pay all the future [[Claim]]s that have been promised. This is the water that has to be paid out. The difference between them is the [[Policyholders Surplus]], which is the cushion, the spare water in The Pool that proves the insurer is [[Solvency|solvent]].

It’s the fundamental report that tells you if The Pool is deep enough or if it's about to run dry.
