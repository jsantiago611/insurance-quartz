---
title: Loss Adjustment Expenses (LAE)
---
[[Loss Adjustment Expenses (LAE)]] are the costs an insurer incurs to investigate, defend, and settle claims. It's the cost of managing the outflow from [[The Pool]].

This is the "cost of doing business" part of paying claims. It's not the claim payment itself; it's everything around it. The salary of the [[Claims Representative]], the fee for the independent adjuster, the cost of a defense lawyer, the court costs. These expenses are a huge part of the [[Loss Ratio]]. In some lines of business, like professional liability, the LAE can be bigger than the actual settlement amount because the legal defense is so expensive. The Pool isn't just paying for losses; it's paying for the bureaucracy required to decide who gets paid.
