--- 
title: Utmost Good Faith
---
Utmost Good Faith is the legal principle that requires both the insurer and the insured to be completely honest and forthright with each other. It's the "no secrets" rule for dealing with [[The Pool]].

And this is the foundational ethic, right? Insurance is a weird contract. The insurer is selling a promise based on information you provide. They can't inspect every inch of your house or your life. So they have to trust you. And you have to trust that they'll be there when you need them. This is why a material misrepresentation on an application can void the entire policy. The whole deal falls apart if you're hiding the fact that you're storing fireworks in your basement. It's what makes an insurance policy more than just a legal form—it's a relationship of trust. Still? It has to be.
