---
title: Independent Exposures
---
[[Independent Exposures]] are loss exposures where the occurrence of a loss from one does not affect the probability of a loss from another.

This is the statistical dream for [[The Pool]]. Think of one house in Florida burning down because of faulty wiring, and another house in Ohio getting hail damage. Totally unrelated, uncorrelated events. This is what lets the law of large numbers work its magic. The Pool can smoothly pay out these random, isolated claims.

The nightmare is the opposite—correlated risk. A hurricane, an earthquake, a pandemic. When exposures aren't independent, The Pool can be wiped out all at once. The entire practice of [[Reinsurance]] and [[Catastrophe Model]]ing is about dealing with the fact that the world often doesn't provide nice, independent exposures.
