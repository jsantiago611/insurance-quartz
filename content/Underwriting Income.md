---
title: Underwriting Income
---
Underwriting Income is the profit (or loss) an insurer makes from its core insurance operations, *before* counting [[Investment Income]]. It's the [[Premium]]s collected minus [[Claim]]s and expenses.

This is the result of the [[Combined Ratio]]. If the combined ratio is under 100%, you have an underwriting profit. You collected more in premiums than you spent on the business of insurance. If it's over 100%, you have an underwriting loss. You're paying out more in claims and expenses than you're taking in, which is okay, I guess, if your [[Investment Income]] is huge enough to cover the gap? But it's not a great long-term strategy. Underwriting income measures how good you are at the actual insurance part of the insurance business.
