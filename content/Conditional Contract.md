---
title: Conditional Contract
---
An insurance policy is a Conditional Contract. This means the insurer's promise to pay is dependent on the insured fulfilling certain duties.

This is the "or else" part of the deal. The policy isn't a gift; it's a trade. You pay the premium, you report claims promptly, you cooperate with the investigation... and then The Pool pays. If you don't hold up your end, they can deny the claim. It's why policies have a whole section called "Duties After a Loss." It's a conditional promise. It's not, "Here's a blank check." It's, "We'll have your back if you follow these rules." This is deeply intertwined with the principle of [[Utmost Good Faith]].
