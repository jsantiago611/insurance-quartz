---
title: Loss Ratio
---
The [[Loss Ratio]] is the percentage of each premium dollar that is spent on claims and the costs of adjusting them ([[Loss Adjustment Expenses (LAE)]]).

This is the other half of the [[Combined Ratio]]. If the loss ratio is 65%, that means 65 cents of every premium dollar went back out the door to claimants and the people who handle their claims. A low loss ratio means the insurer is, on average, collecting more in premiums than it's paying out in losses—that's a good underwriting profit. A high loss ratio, especially one over 100%, means the insurer is paying out more in claims than it's taking in in premiums for that period... which is not sustainable without [[Investment Income]] to bail it out.
